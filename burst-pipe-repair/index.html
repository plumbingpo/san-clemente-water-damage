A burst pipe is one of the most common water damage events a property can face, and potentially one of the most destructive. One broken line can put out 4 to 8 gallons every minute, so 240 to 480 gallons an hour may be running through floors, ceilings and walls. Pipes burst from freezing, old plumbing, too much water pressure, corrosion or a physical hit, and in every case a fast emergency response is what keeps water from ruining flooring, structural materials, building systems and your belongings.

When a pipe bursts in San Clemente or the surrounding area, San Clemente Restoration runs an emergency response around the clock. Our restoration technicians can help you locate and shut off your water supply, and we coordinate with licensed plumbers so the plumbing repair is done properly. Once the water source is stopped, we immediately begin extraction with truck-mounted and portable equipment, removing standing water from all affected areas as quickly as possible.

After the water is out, we dry every material that took on moisture: finished floors, subflooring, wood framing, insulation and drywall. Since water runs downhill through ceiling spaces, floor assemblies and wall cavities, a burst pipe frequently affects several rooms on more than one level. Our thermal imaging and moisture detection equipment traces water migration paths so every affected area is identified and properly dried, which prevents the mold growth and structural deterioration that hidden moisture causes.

Signs It Is Time for Burst Pipe Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A musty smell near a wall, cabinet or floor with plumbing behind it
  • Wet spots, bulging or staining on walls or ceilings
  • Brownish water at the faucet, which can mean corroded pipes
  • The sound of running water when no fixtures are in use
  • Unexplained standing water along baseboards or in the middle of a room
  • Frost or ice on exposed pipes during cold weather
  • Faucets that give only a trickle, or nothing, during a freeze

How to Prevent Burst Pipe Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A plumbing inspection by a licensed pro every few years catches corrosion and wear early
  • Insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas such as attics, garages and crawl spaces
  • Leave the heat on when you travel in winter so indoor pipes stay above freezing
  • Put leak sensors by the water heater and along the main supply line
  • When it freezes, let taps drip and open the cabinets under sinks to let warm air reach the pipes
  • Replace old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes before they fail unexpectedly

What a Professional Brings to Burst Pipe Repair

What you can see after a pipe bursts is usually only part of the damage. Water travels through wall cavities, along electrical conduits, through floor assemblies and between ceiling joists, saturating materials in places you may not discover for days or weeks. By then, mold may already be established in hidden spaces and structural materials may be significantly weakened. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to trace water migration and identify all affected areas immediately.

Professional-grade extraction and drying equipment also works on a completely different scale than consumer tools. A truck-mounted unit clears water at hundreds of times the speed of a shop vac, while a commercial dehumidifier takes 10 to 20 times more moisture out of the air than a household model. That speed and power are essential when you are racing the 24 to 48 hour window before mold growth begins.
