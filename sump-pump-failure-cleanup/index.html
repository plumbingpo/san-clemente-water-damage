Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in San Clemente

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.
