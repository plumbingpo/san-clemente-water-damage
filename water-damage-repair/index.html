Water Damage Repair, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

Confirm It Is Dry

Final moisture readings are taken on the subfloor, framing and any drywall left in place. Only when each material reads normal does rebuilding start, which keeps damp spots from being sealed inside.

2

Write the Scope

Each room is itemized in a document showing replacements, the materials chosen and how long we expect it to take. When there is an open claim, the scope is coordinated with your adjuster.

Why Professional Water Damage Repair Matters

Repair after water damage is not ordinary remodeling. Whoever rebuilds has to understand where the water travelled, what it affected and whether the framing inside the wall has fully dried. Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Take pictures of damaged materials prior to removal, since the insurer may want them later.
  • Save spare tile and flooring, plus paint color details, for matching later repairs.
  • File the written scope and photos of the completed repair with your household papers.
  • Before a contractor closes a floor or wall, request the final moisture numbers.
  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • After drying, the hardwood is still cupped, crowned or lifting.
  • Interior doors now stick or will not latch after swelling from moisture.
  • At floor level, cabinet panels and bases show swelling, peeling or soft spots.
  • Drywall was cut out during drying and the wall cavities are still open.
  • Laminate or vinyl plank edges are swollen, curled or separating at the seams.
